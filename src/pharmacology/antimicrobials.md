# Antimicrobial Agents in Wildlife Medicine

## Introduction

Antimicrobial therapy in wildlife presents unique challenges: limited pharmacokinetic data across species, difficulty maintaining dosing schedules in free-ranging animals, and growing concerns about antimicrobial resistance. Drug selection should be guided by culture and sensitivity whenever possible, though empirical therapy is often initiated while awaiting results.

## Antibiotics

### Beta-Lactams

#### Penicillins
- **Amoxicillin**: broad-spectrum, well-tolerated across most taxa; commonly used for soft tissue infections, respiratory disease, and wound infections
  - Mammal dose: 10-25 mg/kg PO BID
  - Avian dose: 100-150 mg/kg PO BID-TID
  - Reptile dose: 10-22 mg/kg PO or IM SID-BID
- **Amoxicillin-clavulanate**: extended spectrum with beta-lactamase inhibitor; useful for mixed infections
  - Similar dosing to amoxicillin; oral formulation well-accepted
- **Ampicillin**: parenteral formulation useful when oral dosing is not feasible
  - 10-20 mg/kg IM, IV BID-QID (mammals); higher in birds

#### Cephalosporins
- **Ceftiofur**: third-generation cephalosporin with extended gram-negative coverage
  - Widely used in wildlife: 10-20 mg/kg IM SID (mammals and birds)
  - Crystalline free acid (Excede) provides extended duration (single injection effective for several days)
- **Ceftazidime**: third-generation with strong *Pseudomonas* activity
  - Reptiles: 20-40 mg/kg IM every 72 hours (adjusted for ectotherm metabolism)
  - Birds: 50-100 mg/kg IM, IV TID-QID
- **Cephalexin**: first-generation; good gram-positive coverage for skin and soft tissue infections
  - Mammals: 15-30 mg/kg PO BID-TID

### Fluoroquinolones

- **Enrofloxacin**: the most widely used fluoroquinolone in wildlife medicine; broad-spectrum with excellent tissue penetration
  - Mammals: 5-15 mg/kg PO, IM, SC SID-BID
  - Birds: 10-15 mg/kg PO, IM BID (caution: IV administration can cause acute death in some species)
  - Reptiles: 5-10 mg/kg PO, IM SID (every 48h in some species at lower temperatures)
  - **Caution**: causes cartilage damage in growing animals; avoid in juveniles when possible
  - **Caution**: injectable formulation causes tissue necrosis at IM injection sites in some species; dilute or use SC route
- **Marbofloxacin**: similar spectrum to enrofloxacin with potentially better tissue tolerance
  - 2-5 mg/kg PO, SC, IM SID (mammals); 5-10 mg/kg (birds)
- **Ciprofloxacin**: oral human formulation sometimes used; erratic absorption in some species; generally prefer enrofloxacin or marbofloxacin in wildlife

### Tetracyclines

- **Doxycycline**: treatment of choice for chlamydiosis (*Chlamydia psittaci*) in birds and many tick-borne diseases
  - Birds: 25-50 mg/kg PO BID (direct oral administration; avoid mixing with calcium-rich foods)
  - Mammals: 5-10 mg/kg PO BID
  - Reptiles: 10 mg/kg PO SID
  - **Caution**: esophageal stricture reported in cats; potential concern in small felids; administer with food or water flush
  - **Note**: injectable doxycycline (IV) available but causes tissue irritation if given IM or perivascularly
- **Oxytetracycline**: long-acting injectable formulation (LA-200) used in large wildlife
  - 10-20 mg/kg IM; provides sustained levels for 48-72 hours
  - Tissue irritation at injection site is significant; rotate sites

### Aminoglycosides

- **Amikacin**: preferred aminoglycoside for reptiles; less nephrotoxic than gentamicin; excellent gram-negative coverage
  - Reptiles: 5 mg/kg IM loading dose, then 2.5 mg/kg IM every 72 hours
  - Birds: 15-20 mg/kg IM, IV BID-TID
  - Mammals: 10-15 mg/kg IM, SC, IV SID
- **Gentamicin**: potent gram-negative activity but significant nephrotoxicity risk
  - Use with extreme caution, particularly in dehydrated animals or those with renal compromise
  - Reptile dose: 2.5 mg/kg IM every 72 hours (always ensure adequate hydration)
  - Avoid in birds when possible (narrow therapeutic index)
- **General aminoglycoside cautions**:
  - Ensure adequate hydration before and during treatment
  - Monitor renal function if treatment exceeds 5-7 days
  - Ototoxic and nephrotoxic; risk increased with concurrent NSAID use
  - Synergistic with beta-lactams against many gram-negative bacteria

### Macrolides and Azalides

- **Azithromycin**: long tissue half-life allows less frequent dosing; good intracellular penetration
  - Birds: 40-50 mg/kg PO SID for 3-5 days, then every 48-72 hours
  - Mammals: 5-10 mg/kg PO SID for 3-5 days, then every 48-72 hours
  - Reptiles: 10 mg/kg PO every 48-72 hours
  - Useful for: mycobacterial infections (as part of combination therapy), respiratory infections, chlamydiosis
- **Clarithromycin**: similar spectrum to azithromycin; used in mycobacterial combination therapy
  - 15-25 mg/kg PO BID (mammals); 60-85 mg/kg PO BID (birds)
- **Erythromycin**: older macrolide; GI side effects common; generally superseded by azithromycin

### Other Antibiotics

- **Metronidazole**: anaerobic bacteria and protozoa (see also Antiparasitics chapter)
  - Mammals: 15-25 mg/kg PO BID
  - Birds: 20-50 mg/kg PO BID
  - Reptiles: 20 mg/kg PO every 48 hours
  - **Caution**: neurotoxicity at high doses or prolonged use (ataxia, seizures); particularly in birds of prey
- **Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMS)**: broad-spectrum; oral formulation convenient
  - Mammals: 15-30 mg/kg PO BID
  - Birds: 30-100 mg/kg PO BID (wide dose range reflects species variation)
  - Reptiles: 15-25 mg/kg PO SID
  - **Caution**: sulfonamides can cause renal crystalluria in dehydrated patients; ensure adequate hydration
- **Chloramphenicol**: broad-spectrum with excellent CNS penetration; limited use due to human health concerns (aplastic anemia risk to handlers)
  - Rarely used in wildlife practice; appropriate for specific CNS infections when alternatives are not available
  - Banned in food-producing animals in many jurisdictions

## Antifungals

### Azoles

- **Itraconazole**: first-line for aspergillosis in birds; broad-spectrum antifungal
  - Birds: 5-10 mg/kg PO BID
  - Mammals: 5-10 mg/kg PO SID-BID
  - Reptiles: 5 mg/kg PO SID
  - Requires acidic environment for absorption (give with food); capsule formulation preferred over oral solution in some species
  - **Duration**: aspergillosis treatment typically requires 4-8 weeks minimum; monitor with imaging and serology
- **Voriconazole**: second-generation triazole with broader spectrum than itraconazole
  - Birds: 10-12 mg/kg PO BID (some species)
  - **Caution**: toxic to some penguin species and African grey parrots at standard doses — species-specific safety data essential
  - Better CNS penetration than itraconazole
- **Fluconazole**: excellent CNS penetration; useful for systemic mycoses involving the central nervous system
  - Mammals: 5-10 mg/kg PO SID
  - Reptiles: 5 mg/kg PO every 48-72 hours
  - Birds: 5-15 mg/kg PO BID
- **Terbinafine**: allylamine antifungal; used in combination with azoles for refractory aspergillosis
  - Birds: 10-15 mg/kg PO BID-TID
  - Synergistic with azoles against *Aspergillus*

### Amphotericin B

- Polyene antifungal for severe systemic mycoses
- **Conventional formulation**: nephrotoxic; requires IV administration with careful hydration monitoring
  - 1-1.5 mg/kg IV SID diluted in D5W (birds and mammals); give slowly
  - Monitor renal values (uric acid in birds, BUN/creatinine in mammals)
- **Lipid formulations** (liposomal amphotericin): reduced nephrotoxicity but expensive
  - 1-3 mg/kg IV SID-EOD
- **Intratracheal/nebulized**: delivers drug directly to respiratory system for air sac aspergillosis in birds
  - 1 mg/kg diluted in saline, nebulized BID
- **Topical**: clotrimazole for localized fungal lesions; ear and dermal infections

## Antivirals

Antiviral therapy in wildlife is limited but advancing:

- **Acyclovir**: used for herpesvirus infections in chelonians and psittacines
  - Chelonians: 80 mg/kg PO SID for herpesvirus-associated stomatitis
  - Psittacines: 80 mg/kg PO TID for Pacheco's disease (psittacid herpesvirus)
  - Limited bioavailability; high doses required
- **Famciclovir**: prodrug of penciclovir; better bioavailability than acyclovir in some species
  - Explored in chelonians and other reptiles
- **Interferon**: recombinant feline interferon omega has been used experimentally in some wildlife species; limited evidence base

## Species-Specific Considerations

### Birds
- Higher metabolic rates require more frequent dosing than mammals of equivalent size
- Renal portal system may affect drug kinetics for IM injections in the hindlimb (theoretical concern; debated clinically)
- Crop stasis affects oral drug absorption
- Many antibiotics alter gut flora and may cause secondary fungal overgrowth (aspergillosis risk during prolonged antibiotic therapy)

### Reptiles
- Ectothermic metabolism: drug clearance is temperature-dependent; dosing intervals must be extended at lower body temperatures
- Maintain patients within their preferred optimal temperature zone (POTZ) during treatment to ensure predictable drug metabolism
- Renal portal system may affect IM drug kinetics; front-limb injection preferred for drugs primarily eliminated renally
- Aminoglycosides: administer in the forelimb to avoid first-pass renal elimination via the renal portal system

### Amphibians
- Percutaneous drug absorption through permeable skin allows some drugs to be administered as baths
- Water quality and temperature directly affect drug metabolism and absorption
- Limited pharmacokinetic data; extrapolation from reptilian data is common

### Marine Mammals
- Generally follow mammalian dosing with body weight adjustment
- Thick blubber layer affects SC and IM injection depth and absorption
- Oral dosing via fish (hiding medication inside prey items) is the standard route for trained animals
- Renal function adapted for marine environment; consider when using nephrotoxic drugs

## Empirical Antibiotic Selection Guidelines

| Clinical Syndrome | First-Line Empirical Choice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Soft tissue infection/abscess | Amoxicillin-clavulanate or ceftiofur | Culture abscess contents |
| Respiratory (mammal) | Enrofloxacin or doxycycline | Consider mycoplasma, chlamydia |
| Respiratory (bird) | Enrofloxacin + antifungal prophylaxis | Rule out aspergillosis |
| Gram-negative septicemia | Ceftazidime or amikacin + enrofloxacin | Ensure hydration; monitor renal function |
| Shell rot/wound (reptile) | Ceftazidime + topical silver sulfadiazine | Prolonged therapy typically needed |
| Chlamydiosis (avian) | Doxycycline (45 days minimum) | Zoonotic; treat fully |
| Bite wound/mixed infection | Amoxicillin-clavulanate or metronidazole + enrofloxacin | Anaerobic coverage important |

## Culture and Sensitivity

### Importance in Wildlife

Empirical therapy should be adjusted based on culture and sensitivity results whenever possible:

- Wildlife pathogens may have unpredictable resistance patterns
- Environmental bacteria (water, soil) commonly infect wildlife and may carry unusual resistance genes
- Antimicrobial resistance surveillance in wildlife contributes to One Health AMR monitoring
- Submit samples for culture before initiating therapy when feasible

### Interpretation Caveats

- CLSI breakpoints are established for domestic species and human pathogens — applicability to wildlife is assumed but not validated
- Achievable drug concentrations at the site of infection in wildlife species may differ from those assumed by breakpoint determinations
- In vitro susceptibility does not guarantee in vivo efficacy; clinical response remains the ultimate measure
