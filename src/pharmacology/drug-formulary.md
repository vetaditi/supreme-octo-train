# Quick-Reference Drug Formulary

## Introduction

This formulary provides a rapid-access reference for commonly used drugs in wildlife veterinary medicine. Dosages are compiled from published literature, species-specific pharmacokinetic studies, and clinical experience. **All doses should be considered starting points**; individual patient factors (species, body condition, temperature in ectotherms, concurrent disease, hepatic and renal function) must inform final dosing decisions. Cross-reference with individual drug chapters for detailed pharmacology, safety warnings, and species-specific considerations.

## Important Safety Reminders

1. **Ivermectin is contraindicated in chelonians** — use fenbendazole, praziquantel, or pyrantel instead
2. **Diclofenac is lethal to vultures** (*Gyps* spp.) — use meloxicam as the NSAID of choice for all birds
3. **Fenbendazole bone marrow toxicity** — monitor CBC in birds (especially storks, cranes, vultures) during extended courses
4. **Fluoroquinolone injection site necrosis** — dilute enrofloxacin or use SC/PO route; avoid IV in birds
5. **Aminoglycoside nephrotoxicity** — ensure adequate hydration before and during treatment; monitor renal function
6. **Metronidazole neurotoxicity** — limit duration to 7–10 days when possible; reduce dose if CNS signs appear
7. **NSAID and aminoglycoside combination** — increased nephrotoxicity risk; avoid concurrent use
8. **Reptile renal portal system** — inject medications in forelimbs to avoid first-pass renal metabolism
9. **Temperature-dependent drug metabolism in ectotherms** — maintain patients at POTZ; adjust dosing intervals at lower temperatures
10. **Amphibian skin permeability** — rinse gloves before handling; many topical chemicals are absorbed and toxic
