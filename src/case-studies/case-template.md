# Standardized Case Report Template

## Purpose

A standardized case report format ensures consistent documentation across clinicians, facilities, and time. This template is designed specifically for wildlife veterinary cases and accounts for the unique information requirements of free-ranging and captive wild animal patients. Consistent documentation supports clinical auditing, outcomes research, and contributions to shared databases such as the Wildlife Health Information Sharing Partnership (WHISPers).

## Template Structure

### Section 1: Signalment and Intake Information

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Case Number** | Unique facility identifier |
| **Date of Admission** | Date and time |
| **Species** | Common and scientific name |
| **Age Class** | Hatchling/neonate, juvenile, subadult, adult, geriatric (or estimated age) |
| **Sex** | Male, female, undetermined |
| **Body Weight** | In grams or kilograms, as appropriate |
| **Body Condition Score** | Using species-appropriate scale (e.g., 1–5 or 1–9) |
| **Band/Tag/Microchip** | Any identifying marks or devices |
| **Location Found** | GPS coordinates or detailed locality description |
| **Habitat Type** | Forest, wetland, urban, agricultural, coastal, etc. |
| **Finder Information** | Name, contact, relationship to animal |
| **Referring Agency** | Wildlife agency, rehabilitation center, law enforcement |
| **Conservation Status** | IUCN, federal, or state listing |

### Section 2: History and Circumstances of Presentation

Document the following in narrative form:

- **Circumstances of finding** — How and where the animal was discovered; behavior at the time of finding
- **Duration of observation** — How long the animal was observed or held before presentation
- **Interim care** — Any food, water, or treatment provided before admission
- **Environmental context** — Recent weather events, land use changes, pesticide applications, construction, known disease outbreaks in the area
- **Other affected animals** — Whether other animals of the same or different species were found dead or ill nearby
- **Potential exposures** — Known or suspected toxicant, trauma source, or infectious agent exposure
- **Legal context** — Whether the case involves law enforcement (e.g., suspected shooting, poisoning, or illegal take)

### Section 3: Physical Examination Findings

#### General Assessment

- Mentation (alert, depressed, obtunded, stuporous, comatose)
- Posture and locomotion
- Hydration status (skin turgor, mucous membrane moisture, eye position)
- Body condition (pectoral muscle mass, fat stores, overall conformation)
- Temperature (cloacal or rectal, as appropriate)

#### System-by-System Examination

- **Integument** — Feathers/fur/scales/skin condition, wounds, ectoparasites, abnormal growths
- **Musculoskeletal** — Fractures, joint swelling, muscle wasting, range of motion
- **Head/Eyes/Ears/Nose/Throat** — Ocular clarity, pupillary light response, nasal or oral discharge, beak/dental condition
- **Respiratory** — Rate, effort, auscultation findings, air sac sounds (avian)
- **Cardiovascular** — Heart rate, rhythm, pulse quality, capillary refill time
- **Gastrointestinal** — Abdominal palpation, cloacal/rectal examination, fecal character
- **Urogenital** — Cloacal examination, reproductive status if determinable
- **Neurological** — Cranial nerve assessment, proprioception, reflexes, grip strength (raptors)

### Section 4: Diagnostic Workup

#### Imaging

- Radiographs (views obtained, findings)
- Ultrasound (if performed)
- CT/MRI (if available)
- Endoscopy (if performed)

#### Laboratory

- Hematology (PCV/TS minimum; CBC with differential if available)
- Blood chemistry panel
- Blood lead level or other toxicology screening
- Urinalysis (if applicable)

#### Sampling

- Blood cultures
- Swabs (tracheal, cloacal, wound, oral)
- Fecal examination (flotation, direct smear)
- Tissue samples (biopsy, fine needle aspirate)
- Samples archived for future analysis

### Section 5: Problem List

Enumerate each identified problem as a numbered list:

1. Problem one (e.g., "Left humeral fracture, mid-diaphyseal, transverse")
2. Problem two (e.g., "Moderate dehydration, estimated 7%")
3. Problem three (e.g., "Elevated blood lead, 0.48 ppm")

### Section 6: Differential Diagnoses

For each problem, list differential diagnoses ranked by likelihood:

| Problem | Differential 1 (most likely) | Differential 2 | Differential 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fracture | Vehicular trauma | Gunshot | Window strike |
| Elevated lead | Ingested lead ammunition fragments | Lead fishing tackle | Environmental contamination |

### Section 7: Treatment Plan

- **Immediate stabilization** — Fluid therapy, thermal support, pain management, wound management
- **Definitive treatment** — Surgical plan, chelation protocol, antimicrobial therapy, supportive care
- **Medications** — Drug, dose, route, frequency, duration
- **Nutritional support** — Diet type, feeding schedule, caloric targets
- **Housing** — Enclosure type, substrate, temperature, lighting
- **Monitoring plan** — Parameters to track, frequency of reassessment
- **Rehabilitation milestones** — Criteria for progression through rehabilitation stages
- **Release criteria** — Specific functional benchmarks required for release

### Section 8: Outcome

- **Disposition** — Released, transferred, permanent captivity, died, euthanized
- **Date of disposition**
- **Release location** (GPS coordinates preferred)
- **Post-release monitoring** — Transmitter attached, banding data, follow-up plan
- **Necropsy findings** (if applicable, with reference to full necropsy report)

### Section 9: Discussion and Lessons Learned

- Key clinical takeaways
- What went well and what could be improved
- Relevance to other cases or species
- Literature comparison
- Recommendations for protocol changes

## Filled Example Case

### Section 1: Signalment and Intake Information

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Case Number** | WRC-2025-0347 |
| **Date of Admission** | 15 March 2025, 14:30 |
| **Species** | Red-tailed hawk (*Buteo jamaicensis*) |
| **Age Class** | Adult (after second-year plumage) |
| **Sex** | Female |
| **Body Weight** | 1,080 g |
| **Body Condition Score** | 2/5 (thin) |
| **Band/Tag/Microchip** | USGS aluminum band #1234-56789 |
| **Location Found** | 38.9072 N, 77.0369 W; roadside ditch along Route 29 |
| **Habitat Type** | Suburban/agricultural interface |
| **Finder Information** | Jane Smith, local resident |
| **Referring Agency** | Virginia Department of Wildlife Resources |
| **Conservation Status** | Not listed; protected under MBTA |

### Section 2: History and Circumstances

Found by the side of a two-lane highway, unable to fly. Finder observed the bird for approximately two hours before contacting the wildlife agency. No interim care was provided. The bird was found within 50 meters of a recently harvested corn field. No other dead or sick birds were reported in the area. The county has documented cases of lead exposure in raptors associated with hunting season (recently ended).

### Section 3: Physical Examination

- **Mentation**: Depressed but responsive to handling
- **Hydration**: Mildly dehydrated (approximately 5%), mild enophthalmos
- **Temperature**: 40.1 C cloacal
- **Integument**: No external wounds; feathers in good condition aside from soiling
- **Musculoskeletal**: Palpable instability of left humerus at mid-shaft; crepitus present; no other fractures detected. Mild pectoral muscle wasting.
- **Eyes**: Both eyes clear, PLR normal bilaterally, no fundic abnormalities
- **Respiratory**: Rate 28/min, no abnormal sounds on auscultation
- **Cardiovascular**: Heart rate 320 bpm, strong femoral pulse, CRT < 2 sec
- **GI**: Crop empty, no palpable abnormalities
- **Neurological**: Alert, appropriate responses; mild weakness in left foot grip compared to right

### Section 4: Diagnostic Workup

- **Radiographs**: VD and lateral whole-body. Transverse mid-diaphyseal fracture of left humerus with minimal displacement. No metallic densities in GI tract. No evidence of gunshot.
- **Hematology**: PCV 38%, TP 4.2 g/dL, WBC 14,000/uL with mild heterophilia
- **Blood chemistry**: Glucose 280 mg/dL, uric acid 12 mg/dL (mildly elevated), AST 320 U/L (elevated), CK 2,800 U/L (elevated consistent with muscle injury)
- **Blood lead**: 0.48 ppm (consistent with clinical lead poisoning; >0.2 ppm considered elevated)
- **Fecal float**: Rare Capillaria ova

### Section 5: Problem List

1. Transverse mid-diaphyseal fracture of the left humerus
2. Clinical lead poisoning (blood lead 0.48 ppm)
3. Mild dehydration (5%)
4. Underweight (BCS 2/5)
5. Low-grade Capillaria infection

### Section 6: Differential Diagnoses

| Problem | Differential 1 | Differential 2 | Differential 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Humeral fracture | Vehicular collision (location, fracture type) | Lead-induced weakness leading to ground impact | Collision with structure |
| Elevated lead | Ingestion of lead ammunition in prey/carrion | Environmental lead exposure | Lead fishing tackle |

### Section 7: Treatment Plan

- **Fluids**: LRS 50 mL/kg/day SC divided BID for 48 hours, then reassess
- **Analgesia**: Meloxicam 0.5 mg/kg PO SID
- **Chelation**: CaEDTA 35 mg/kg IM BID for 5 days; recheck blood lead at day 5
- **Fracture management**: Figure-of-eight bandage and body wrap for initial stabilization; surgical repair (IM pin) planned after chelation course if blood lead decreases appropriately
- **Nutrition**: Whole prey items (mice) offered once hydration corrected; target 150 kcal/day
- **Antiparasitic**: Fenbendazole 25 mg/kg PO SID for 5 days for Capillaria
- **Housing**: Indoor critical care unit, padded perching, dim lighting to minimize stress

### Section 8: Outcome

Chelation therapy reduced blood lead to 0.12 ppm by day 5. IM pin placement was performed on day 7 under isoflurane anesthesia. Post-surgical radiographs confirmed good alignment. Pin was removed at week 6 with radiographic evidence of complete callus formation. The bird progressed through flight conditioning over four additional weeks and achieved sustained flight with appropriate maneuverability. Released at the original finding location on 10 May 2025 with the existing USGS band. No post-release monitoring transmitter was available.

### Section 9: Discussion and Lessons Learned

This case illustrates the common co-occurrence of lead poisoning and trauma in raptors during and after hunting season. The decision to address lead toxicosis before surgical fracture repair was appropriate, as elevated lead levels can impair bone healing and increase anesthetic risk. The source of lead exposure was most likely consumption of carrion or prey containing lead ammunition fragments, consistent with regional epidemiological data. This case supports the value of routine blood lead screening in all raptor admissions, regardless of primary presenting complaint.
