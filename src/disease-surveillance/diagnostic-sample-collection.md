# Diagnostic Sample Collection in Wildlife

## Introduction

The quality of diagnostic results depends entirely on the quality of specimens submitted. Wildlife sample collection presents unique challenges: remote field locations, limited equipment, environmental extremes, diverse anatomy across taxa, and the physiological effects of capture stress. This chapter provides practical guidance on specimen collection, handling, preservation, and transport for wildlife diagnostic investigations.

## Blood Collection

### General Principles

- Collect blood as early as possible after capture to minimize stress-related artifacts
- Use appropriate anticoagulants for the intended analyses
- Avoid hemolysis through gentle handling, appropriate needle gauge, and proper technique
- Label all tubes immediately with species, individual ID, date, time, and location

### Tube Types and Applications

| Tube Type | Anticoagulant | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| Red top (plain) | None | Serum — serology, chemistry |
| Purple/lavender top | EDTA | Hematology, PCR, genetics |
| Green top | Lithium heparin | Plasma chemistry (preferred for reptiles and birds) |
| Blue top | Sodium citrate | Coagulation studies |
| Gray top | Sodium fluoride | Glucose, lactate |

**Note**: lithium heparin is preferred over EDTA for reptilian and avian plasma chemistry because EDTA can cause hemolysis in some species.

### Venipuncture Sites by Taxon

#### Mammals
- **Small mammals (rodents, bats, insectivores)**: saphenous vein, retro-orbital sinus (terminal), cardiac puncture (terminal only, under anesthesia), tail vein (rodents)
- **Medium mammals (raccoons, foxes, mustelids)**: jugular vein, cephalic vein, saphenous vein, femoral vein
- **Large mammals (deer, elk, bears)**: jugular vein (primary), auricular vein, cephalic or saphenous vein
- **Marine mammals — pinnipeds**: epidural venous sinus (dorsal approach), caudal gluteal vein, flipper interdigital veins
- **Marine mammals — cetaceans**: ventral fluke veins (periarterial venous rete)

#### Birds
- **Right jugular vein**: primary site for most birds; the right jugular is larger than the left in most avian species
- **Basilic (ulnar/wing) vein**: accessible, commonly used; risk of hematoma — apply firm pressure post-collection
- **Medial metatarsal vein**: useful in larger birds (raptors, waterfowl, wading birds)
- **Toenail clip**: limited volume, used for blood smears and small-volume PCR in passerines; causes discomfort and should be used only when venipuncture is not feasible

#### Reptiles
- **Chelonians (turtles, tortoises)**: subcarapacial (dorsal cervical) sinus, jugular vein, brachial vein/sinus, coccygeal (ventral tail) vein
- **Lizards**: ventral coccygeal (tail) vein, ventral abdominal vein (under anesthesia)
- **Snakes**: ventral coccygeal vein (posterior to cloaca), cardiac puncture (under anesthesia, with ultrasound guidance), palatine veins
- **Crocodilians**: supravertebral vessel (dorsal approach behind occiput), ventral coccygeal vein

#### Amphibians
- **Ventral abdominal vein** (midline, under anesthesia)
- **Femoral vein**
- **Cardiac puncture** (terminal, under anesthesia)
- **Lingual plexus** (in larger species)
- Volumes are limited; maximum safe collection is approximately 1% of body weight

#### Fish
- **Caudal vein/artery** (ventral approach, posterior to anal fin)
- **Cardiac puncture** (under anesthesia)
- **Gill arch vessels** (limited volume)

### Blood Volume Limits

Safe maximum blood collection volumes:

| Taxon | Maximum Volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mammals | 1% of body weight (10 mL/kg) | From healthy animals; reduce for compromised |
| Birds | 1% of body weight | Equivalent to ~10% of blood volume |
| Reptiles | 0.5-0.8% of body weight | Conservative approach recommended |
| Amphibians | 1% of body weight | Very small absolute volumes |

## Swab Collection

### Oropharyngeal Swabs
- Insert sterile swab into the oral cavity, rotating along the pharyngeal mucosa
- Used for: respiratory virus detection (avian influenza, paramyxoviruses), bacterial culture, choanal cytology (birds)
- Place in viral transport media (VTM) for virology or appropriate transport media for bacteriology

### Cloacal/Rectal Swabs
- Insert swab gently into cloaca (birds, reptiles) or rectum (mammals)
- Used for: enteric pathogen detection (Salmonella, avian influenza, enteric viruses)
- Place in VTM, Cary-Blair media (enteric bacteria), or other specified transport media

### Nasal Swabs
- Useful for respiratory pathogen screening in mammals
- Mini-tip swabs for small species
- Place in VTM or universal transport media (UTM)

### Skin/Lesion Swabs
- Swab active lesion margins for dermatophyte culture
- Skin swabs for *Batrachochytrium* PCR in amphibians — standardized swabbing protocol (ventral surfaces, feet)
- Swab exudates from abscesses, wounds, or discharge

## Tissue Collection

### Fresh Tissues
- Collect aseptically into sterile containers or Whirl-Pak bags
- Keep on ice (not frozen) for bacteriology, virology, and toxicology
- Freeze at -20C or -80C for molecular diagnostics and toxicology archives
- Collect representative samples of all major organs during necropsy: brain, lung, heart, liver, spleen, kidney, gastrointestinal tract, lymph nodes, gonads

### Fixed Tissues
- Collect tissue sections no thicker than 1 cm for adequate fixation
- Place in 10% neutral buffered formalin at a 10:1 formalin-to-tissue ratio
- Allow a minimum of 24-48 hours for fixation before shipping
- Collect both grossly normal and abnormal tissue from each organ
- Decalcification may be needed for bone samples

### Specialized Tissue Collection

| Purpose | Tissue | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Rabies testing | Brain (hippocampus, cerebellum, brainstem) | Fresh, refrigerated, NOT frozen, NOT in formalin |
| CWD testing | Retropharyngeal lymph nodes, obex | Fresh or in formalin (confirm with testing lab) |
| Toxicology | Liver, kidney, fat, GI contents, blood | Frozen in chemically clean containers |
| Genetics | Muscle, skin, blood (EDTA) | Frozen or in 95% ethanol or DMSO buffer |
| Electron microscopy | Small (1 mm) tissue cubes | Glutaraldehyde fixation |

## Fecal and Urine Collection

### Fecal Samples
- Collect fresh feces directly from the animal or from a clean surface immediately after defecation
- For parasitology: place in sealed container, refrigerate, examine within 24-48 hours; or preserve in 10% formalin or SAF (sodium acetate-acetic acid-formalin)
- For bacteriology: use appropriate transport media (Cary-Blair for enteric pathogens)
- For molecular diagnostics: freeze at -20C or -80C
- For dietary analysis: freeze or dry

### Urine Collection
- Free-catch during handling (common in stressed animals)
- Cystocentesis (ultrasound-guided when possible)
- Catheterization (species-dependent feasibility)
- For leptospirosis PCR: collect in sterile container, refrigerate, process within 24 hours

## Handling and Preservation

### Cold Chain Management

Maintaining an unbroken cold chain is critical for many diagnostic specimens:

- **Ice packs (4C)**: bacteriology, serology, most fresh tissues (short-term)
- **Dry ice (-78C)**: virology samples requiring ultra-cold storage during transport
- **Liquid nitrogen (-196C)**: cryopreservation of tissues for cell culture or specialized analysis
- **Ambient temperature**: formalin-fixed tissues, blood smears (after drying), fecal samples in preservative

### Fixatives

| Fixative | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 10% neutral buffered formalin | Histopathology | Standard for tissue fixation; 10:1 ratio |
| 95% ethanol | Genetics, molecular | Does not cross-link DNA; alternative to freezing |
| Glutaraldehyde (2.5%) | Electron microscopy | Very small samples; cold storage |
| SAF (sodium acetate-acetic acid-formalin) | Fecal parasitology | Preserves parasite morphology |
| 70% ethanol | Ectoparasite preservation | Maintain specimen morphology for identification |
| DMSO salt-saturated | Genetics | Room temperature stable for extended periods |
| FTA cards | Blood for DNA/RNA | Dried blood spots; lightweight, ambient transport |

### Blood Smear Preparation

- Prepare smears immediately after blood collection in the field
- Use a clean, unchipped slide at a 30-45 degree angle
- Air-dry rapidly (wave gently or use fan); do not heat-fix
- Fix in methanol within 4 hours or protect from humidity
- Stain with Wright-Giemsa, Diff-Quik, or equivalent
- Prepare at least 3-4 smears per animal; label with pencil on frosted end

## Labeling and Documentation

### Sample Labeling Requirements
- Species (common and scientific name)
- Individual ID (band number, tag number, or unique identifier)
- Date and time of collection
- Location (GPS coordinates preferred)
- Sample type and tissue
- Collector name and contact information
- Preservative or anticoagulant used
- Any relevant clinical observations

### Chain of Custody
For forensic or regulatory cases, maintain chain of custody documentation:
- Continuous record of who possessed the sample, when, and where
- Tamper-evident packaging
- Secure storage between collection and submission
- Written transfer records at each handoff

## Transport Regulations

### Domestic Shipping
- Follow IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations for formalin and dry ice
- Package diagnostic specimens according to UN 3373 (Biological Substance, Category B) or UN 2814/2900 (Category A for known high-consequence pathogens)
- Triple packaging required: primary watertight container, secondary watertight container with absorbent material, rigid outer packaging
- Include specimen submission forms and any required permits

### International Shipping
- Import permits may be required for biological specimens
- CITES permits required for samples from listed species
- Comply with Nagoya Protocol requirements for genetic resources from signatory countries
- Use approved courier services experienced in biological specimen transport
- Declare contents accurately on customs forms

## Biosafety During Collection

### Personal Protective Equipment

Minimum PPE for wildlife sample collection:

| Risk Level | PPE Requirements |
|---|---|
| Standard | Nitrile gloves, eye protection, field clothing |
| Elevated (necropsy, known pathogens) | Double gloves, N95 respirator, eye protection, coveralls or Tyvek suit, rubber boots |
| High (BSL-3 pathogens suspected) | Powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR), double gloves, fluid-resistant coveralls, boot covers, face shield |

### Decontamination
- Disinfect work surfaces and equipment between animals
- Effective disinfectants for most pathogens: 10% bleach, quaternary ammonium compounds, accelerated hydrogen peroxide
- Prions require special decontamination: 2N NaOH for 1 hour or autoclaving at 134C for 18 minutes
- Dispose of sharps in puncture-resistant containers
- Bag and seal all contaminated waste for appropriate disposal

### Zoonotic Risk Mitigation
- Never eat, drink, or smoke during specimen collection
- Cover any open wounds on hands before gloving
- Avoid needlestick injuries through careful technique and communication during team procedures
- Wash hands thoroughly after removing gloves
- Report any exposure incidents immediately and seek medical evaluation
