# Appendices

## Introduction

The appendices of this text provide reference materials, quick-access tools, and supplementary resources designed to support the wildlife veterinarian in both clinical practice and field operations. While the preceding chapters present detailed didactic content on the principles and practice of wildlife veterinary medicine, these appendices serve as a practical toolkit — the materials you reach for when you need a drug dose in the field, a reference range for an unfamiliar species, or a checklist to ensure your kit is complete before deploying to a remote site.

## Purpose and Scope

Wildlife veterinary practice demands rapid access to information across an exceptionally broad range of species, disciplines, and contexts. The wildlife veterinarian may examine a bald eagle in the morning, assist with an elk capture operation in the afternoon, and process samples from a bat mortality event in the evening. No clinician can maintain all relevant reference data in memory, and the consequences of errors — wrong drug doses, misinterpreted laboratory values, missing equipment — can be severe for both the animal and the veterinarian.

These appendices are designed to be consulted quickly and repeatedly. They are organized for practical use rather than narrative flow, with tables, checklists, and concise definitions that support decision-making at the point of care.

## Contents

This section contains the following appendices:

### 1. Glossary

A comprehensive glossary of key terms used throughout this text and in the broader wildlife veterinary literature. Entries span clinical terminology, ecological concepts, regulatory terms, and field-specific vocabulary that may be unfamiliar to veterinarians entering wildlife practice from domestic animal backgrounds. Terms are arranged alphabetically with concise, precise definitions.

### 2. Reference Ranges

Hematology and serum biochemistry reference ranges for major wildlife taxa, organized by taxonomic group. These tables provide baseline values for interpreting clinical pathology results, with the critical caveat that reference ranges in wildlife are inherently approximate — they are derived from limited sample sizes, may not account for seasonal variation, age, sex, reproductive status, or capture stress effects, and should be interpreted with caution. Where possible, the source and sample context are noted.

The reference ranges cover:
- Raptors (eagles, hawks, owls, falcons)
- Waterbirds (waterfowl, shorebirds, wading birds)
- Passerines (songbirds)
- Large mammals (ungulates, large carnivores)
- Small mammals (rodents, lagomorphs, insectivores, bats)
- Primates (non-human primates)
- Marine mammals (cetaceans, pinnipeds)
- Reptiles (chelonians, squamates, crocodilians)

### 3. Equipment Checklists

Itemized checklists for the essential equipment required in common wildlife veterinary operations. These checklists serve both as preparation guides before deployment and as inventory tools for maintaining field readiness. Checklists are provided for:

- General field kit
- Capture equipment
- Anesthesia kit
- Necropsy kit
- Sample collection kit
- Rehabilitation supplies

Each checklist uses a checkbox format suitable for printing and lamination for field use.

### 4. Resources and References

A curated compilation of key resources for the wildlife veterinary professional, including:

- Foundational textbooks and clinical references
- Peer-reviewed journals publishing wildlife veterinary research
- Online databases and information systems
- Professional organizations and their membership benefits
- Disease reporting systems and contacts
- Emergency contacts and hotlines

## How to Use These Appendices

- **In the field**: print or download the equipment checklists and reference ranges to a mobile device; laminated pocket cards are particularly useful for frequently consulted tables
- **In the clinic**: use the reference ranges as a starting point for clinical pathology interpretation, but always seek species-specific literature when available
- **For study**: the glossary provides a foundation for understanding the interdisciplinary vocabulary of wildlife veterinary medicine
- **For networking**: the resources appendix connects you with the professional community and the literature that will sustain your continuing education

## Limitations

These appendices are reference tools, not substitutes for clinical judgment, current literature review, or consultation with experienced colleagues. Reference ranges are approximations. Checklists must be adapted to specific operations. Contact information and organizational details are subject to change. The wildlife veterinarian should view these materials as a foundation to be supplemented by ongoing professional development and species-specific expertise.
