# Sample Collection and Preservation

## Overview

Proper sample collection and preservation are critical to maximizing the diagnostic yield of a wildlife necropsy. Poorly collected or improperly preserved samples can render even the most thorough gross examination inconclusive. This chapter provides detailed guidance on collecting samples for histopathology, microbiology, toxicology, parasitology, cytology, and molecular diagnostics, as well as regulations governing sample shipment.

## Tissue Sampling for Histopathology

### Formalin Fixation

**Fixative preparation**: 10% neutral buffered formalin (NBF) is the standard fixative for routine histopathology.

- Commercial 10% NBF is a 1:10 dilution of concentrated (37-40%) formaldehyde in phosphate-buffered saline
- To prepare from concentrated formalin: 100 mL of 37% formaldehyde + 900 mL distilled water + 4 g monobasic sodium phosphate + 6.5 g dibasic sodium phosphate
- pH should be 6.8-7.2; unbuffered formalin causes formalin pigment artifact

**Formalin-to-tissue ratio**: A minimum of 10:1 (formalin volume to tissue volume) is essential for adequate fixation. A 20:1 ratio is preferable:

| Tissue Volume | Minimum Formalin Volume | Preferred Formalin Volume |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cm^3 | 10 mL | 20 mL |
| 10 cm^3 | 100 mL | 200 mL |
| 50 cm^3 | 500 mL | 1000 mL |

**Tissue thickness**: Sections should be no more than 0.5-1.0 cm thick to allow formalin penetration. Formalin penetrates tissue at approximately 1 mm per hour.

**Fixation time**: Minimum 24-48 hours for standard tissues. Brain requires 7-14 days in formalin before sectioning. Overfixation (months to years) is generally preferable to underfixation for routine histopathology, though it may compromise some immunohistochemistry protocols.

### Standard Tissue Collection Set

The following tissues should be collected routinely for a complete histopathological examination:

**Minimum tissue set (all species)**:
- Lung (sample from each lobe or representative sections from multiple regions)
- Heart (sections of left ventricle, right ventricle, interventricular septum)
- Liver (multiple sections from different lobes)
- Kidney (longitudinal section including cortex, medulla, and pelvis)
- Spleen
- Gastrointestinal tract (stomach/proventriculus/ventriculus, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, colon)
- Brain (multiple coronal sections)
- Adrenal gland
- Thyroid
- Lymph nodes (multiple locations)
- Skeletal muscle
- Bone marrow (rib or sternum section)
- Any gross lesions (with adjacent normal tissue for comparison)
- Skin (including any lesions)

**Additional tissues by taxon**:

| Taxon | Additional Tissues |
|---|---|
| Birds | Air sacs, crop, proventriculus, ventriculus, bursa of Fabricius, uropygial gland, cere, tracheal syrinx |
| Reptiles | Fat bodies, spectacle (snakes), salt gland (marine species), shell (chelonians) |
| Amphibians | Skin (multiple sites including ventral pelvic patch), fat bodies |
| Fish | Gills (on cartilaginous arch), head kidney, swim bladder, pyloric ceca, skin/scales |

### Tissue Collection Technique

- Use sharp blades to minimize crush artifact
- Handle tissue gently; avoid excessive squeezing with forceps
- Place tissue in formalin immediately after collection (do not allow drying)
- Include a margin of normal tissue adjacent to any lesion
- Label cassettes or bags clearly with organ name, animal ID, and date
- For large organs, incise (bread-loaf) the tissue at 0.5-1.0 cm intervals before immersion to enhance fixation

## Microbiology Samples

### Bacterial Culture

- Collect samples aseptically before the gastrointestinal tract is opened
- Use sterile swabs with appropriate transport media (Amies, Stuart, or Cary-Blair for enteric organisms)
- Priority sites: lung, liver, spleen, kidney, brain (for systemic infections); site-specific sampling for localized infections
- Refrigerate samples (4 degrees C) if transport will be delayed; do not freeze
- Note: Post-mortem bacterial invasion of tissues begins within hours of death, complicating interpretation. Pure growth of a pathogen from multiple organs supports significance; mixed flora growth is often overgrowth artifact.

### Viral Isolation and Detection

- Collect fresh tissue samples (lung, liver, spleen, brain, intestine, and any lesions) and place in viral transport media or snap-freeze in liquid nitrogen or on dry ice
- Swabs in viral transport media for respiratory and enteric viruses
- Ship on wet ice (for short transport) or dry ice (for longer transport)
- Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles

### Fungal Culture

- Collect tissue samples from lesions and submit fresh or frozen
- For suspected aspergillosis: submit air sac, lung, and any granulomatous lesions
- Dermatophyte suspects: submit skin and hair/feather samples on dermatophyte test medium or in paper envelopes (not sealed plastic, which promotes bacterial overgrowth)

## Toxicology Samples

### General Principles

- Collect samples before formalin fixation (formalin-fixed tissue is unsuitable for most toxicological analyses)
- Wrap samples in aluminum foil and place in clean plastic bags (some analytes may bind to plastic; consult the testing laboratory)
- Freeze samples at -20 degrees C or colder until shipment
- Avoid contamination from gloves, containers, or other sources
- Collect from uncontaminated sites on the animal (internal organs preferred)
- Collect environmental samples when possible (water, soil, bait material, potential poison source)

### Tissue Selection by Toxicant Class

| Toxicant Class | Priority Tissues | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy metals (Pb, Hg, Cd) | Liver, kidney, bone, blood, feather/hair | Liver and kidney are primary accumulation sites; bone for chronic lead; feather/hair for mercury |
| Organochlorines (DDT, PCBs) | Fat (subcutaneous, omental), liver, brain, egg | Lipophilic compounds concentrate in fat; report on lipid-weight basis |
| Cholinesterase inhibitors (OPs, carbamates) | Brain, blood (whole), eye fluid, GI contents | Brain and blood ChE activity; collect on ice, not frozen for ChE assays |
| Anticoagulant rodenticides | Liver (minimum 10 g), blood, GI contents | Liver is the primary site; second-generation compounds are more persistent |
| Cyanide | GI contents, blood, liver, muscle | Collect in airtight containers; cyanide is volatile |
| Botulinum toxin | Serum, GI contents, liver | Submit chilled (not frozen) for mouse bioassay or ELISA |
| Petroleum/oil | Feathers/fur, liver, GI contents, lung | Collect affected integument in foil; PAH metabolites in bile |
| PFAS | Liver, blood/serum, kidney, egg | Collect in polypropylene containers; avoid PTFE/Teflon-containing materials |
| Pharmaceuticals | Liver, kidney, blood/serum, GI contents | Consult laboratory for specific storage requirements |
| Mycotoxins | Liver, GI contents, feed/food source | Aflatoxins, fumonisins, etc. |
| Lead ammunition | GI contents (including any fragments), liver, kidney, blood, bone | Radiograph GI tract before opening to document fragments |

### Minimum Tissue Quantities for Toxicology

- **Liver**: 50-100 g preferred; minimum 10-20 g for most analyses
- **Kidney**: 20-50 g preferred
- **Brain**: One hemisphere (fresh/frozen); other hemisphere in formalin for histopathology
- **Blood**: As much as possible; minimum 3-5 mL whole blood and 3-5 mL serum/plasma
- **Fat**: 20-50 g preferred for lipophilic compound analysis
- **GI contents**: Entire contents if possible, particularly for poisoning investigations
- **Feather/hair**: Multiple samples from defined body regions

## Parasitology

### Ectoparasite Collection

- Collect ectoparasites (ticks, fleas, lice, mites, hippoboscid flies) into 70% ethanol with collection data (host species, body location, date)
- Examine skin surface before skinning; some ectoparasites leave the host rapidly post-mortem
- Note ectoparasite burden estimate (light, moderate, heavy) and distribution
- Preserve representative specimens; note if any are engorged (ticks)

### Endoparasite Collection

- **Gastrointestinal parasites**: Open the entire GI tract and examine contents for helminths. Collect adult worms in 70% ethanol or 10% formalin. Wash intestinal contents through a series of sieves (1 mm, 0.5 mm, 0.15 mm) to recover small parasites.
- **Fecal examination**: Collect fresh feces from the rectum/cloaca for flotation and sedimentation
- **Respiratory parasites**: Open airways and examine for lung worms; examine air sacs in birds
- **Blood parasites**: Prepare thin and thick blood smears; collect EDTA blood for molecular detection
- **Tissue parasites**: Collect tissues with visible parasites or cysts in both formalin (for histology) and 70% ethanol (for morphological identification). Muscle for *Trichinella* examination should be submitted fresh or in pepsin-HCl digest.

### Parasite Identification

- Photograph parasites in situ before removal
- Record location, number, and condition of parasites
- Preserve morphological voucher specimens separately from bulk samples
- Consider molecular identification (PCR) for species-level determination when morphology is insufficient

## Cytology

- Impression smears (touch preps) from cut surfaces of fresh organs provide rapid diagnostic information
- Air-dry smears and stain with Diff-Quik or Wright-Giemsa
- Priority organs for impression cytology: liver, spleen, kidney, lung, lymph nodes, any lesions
- Fluid samples (pleural, peritoneal, pericardial, joint): prepare direct smears and cytocentrifuge preparations
- Bone marrow: aspirate from femur or humerus; prepare smears as for blood

## Molecular Diagnostics

### Sample Collection for PCR and Sequencing

- Collect fresh tissue samples (3-5 mm cubes) in cryovials
- Snap-freeze in liquid nitrogen or place directly on dry ice
- Alternatively, place in RNA/DNA preservation buffer (e.g., RNAlater for RNA preservation; 95% ethanol for DNA)
- Avoid formalin-fixed tissue for molecular diagnostics when possible (formalin cross-links and fragments nucleic acids), though FFPE extraction protocols are available for archival samples

### Priority Tissues for Molecular Testing

| Pathogen Target | Priority Tissues | Preservation |
|---|---|---|
| Respiratory viruses | Lung, trachea, nasal turbinates, air sac (birds) | Frozen or viral transport media |
| Enteric pathogens | Intestinal tissue and contents, cloaca/rectal swabs | Frozen or transport media |
| Systemic viruses | Spleen, liver, kidney, lung | Frozen |
| Chytrid fungi (Bd/Bsal) | Skin swabs (ventral surfaces) | 95% ethanol or dry swab frozen |
| Mycobacteria | Lung, lymph nodes, lesions | Frozen (not in formalin for culture) |
| Prions (CWD) | Obex (brainstem), retropharyngeal lymph nodes, tonsil | Fresh frozen and formalin-fixed |
| Hemoparasites | EDTA blood, spleen | EDTA blood refrigerated; spleen frozen |

### Emerging Metagenomic Approaches

Unbiased metagenomic sequencing (next-generation sequencing of all nucleic acids in a sample) is increasingly used for:

- Detection of unexpected or novel pathogens
- Characterization of the microbiome in health and disease
- Pathogen discovery in mass mortality events of unknown cause
- Requires high-quality nucleic acid extraction; sample preservation is critical

## Sample Shipping Regulations

### IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations

Diagnostic specimens shipped by air must comply with International Air Transport Association (IATA) regulations:

**Category A (UN 2814 / UN 2900)**: Substances known or reasonably expected to contain pathogens that can cause permanent disability, life-threatening, or fatal disease in otherwise healthy humans or animals. Requires Packing Instruction 620.

**Category B (UN 3373)**: Diagnostic specimens that do not meet Category A criteria. Most wildlife diagnostic samples fall into this category. Requires Packing Instruction 650.

**Exempt specimens**: Specimens with minimal likelihood of containing pathogens. Minimal packaging requirements.

### Packaging Requirements (Category B / UN 3373)

The triple packaging system is required:

1. **Primary container**: Leak-proof vial, tube, or bag containing the specimen. Must be wrapped in absorbent material sufficient to absorb the entire contents.
2. **Secondary container**: Leak-proof, durable container (e.g., sealed bag or container) holding the wrapped primary container(s).
3. **Outer packaging**: Rigid shipping container meeting IATA drop-test standards. Must be marked "Biological Substance, Category B" with the UN 3373 diamond mark.

### Additional Requirements

- **Temperature maintenance**: Include sufficient cold packs, wet ice, or dry ice to maintain required temperature during transit. Dry ice requires separate UN 1845 labeling and quantity declaration.
- **Documentation**: Shipping papers, customs declarations (international), permits (CITES, USDA, Fish and Wildlife import/export permits as applicable)
- **Shipper training**: Personnel who package and ship diagnostic specimens must complete dangerous goods training and maintain current certification

### US Department of Transportation (DOT) Regulations

Ground shipment of biological specimens within the United States follows DOT regulations (49 CFR 173.134):

- Category B specimens follow similar triple packaging requirements as IATA
- Exempt specimens have reduced requirements
- State and institutional regulations may impose additional requirements

### Permit Requirements

International shipment of wildlife samples may require:

- **CITES permit**: For samples from CITES-listed species crossing international borders
- **USDA/APHIS import permit**: For animal pathogens and certain diagnostic specimens entering the US
- **CDC import permit**: For specimens potentially containing select agents or zoonotic pathogens
- **Institutional biosafety committee approval**: For work involving certain pathogens
- **Country-specific import permits**: Requirements vary by destination country; consult the receiving laboratory and relevant authorities well in advance

### Practical Recommendations

- Contact the receiving laboratory before shipping to confirm sample requirements, shipping address, and any special instructions
- Ship early in the week (Monday-Wednesday) to avoid weekend delays
- Include a detailed packing list and submission form with every shipment
- Retain copies of all shipping documents and tracking numbers
- For international shipments, allow 4-8 weeks lead time for permit processing
- Use overnight or express shipping services for perishable diagnostic samples
